Glossary

Insurance words, translated.

Short, plain-language definitions of the terms you meet when comparing coverage and benefits.

Glossary terms

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ

21 terms

A
Allowance
A set amount an employer contributes each month toward an employee’s coverage, under a reimbursement arrangement.
Annual maximum
The most a dental or vision plan will pay in a plan year. Costs above it are yours.
C
Coinsurance
Your percentage share of a covered bill after the deductible — for example, 20% while the plan pays 80%.
Copay
A fixed amount you pay for a covered service, such as $25 for a primary care visit.
D
Deductible
What you pay for covered services each year before the plan begins to share costs.
Dependant
A person, such as a child or spouse, who is covered under someone else’s plan.
F
Formulary
A plan’s list of covered medicines, usually grouped into tiers with different costs.
I
In network
Doctors, hospitals and pharmacies that have agreed prices with a plan. Usually the lower-cost choice.
M
Metal level
A label such as Bronze, Silver, Gold or Platinum describing how a plan splits costs between premium and care.
N
Network
The group of providers a plan works with. Staying in network typically keeps your costs lower.
O
Open enrollment
A yearly window when people can sign up for or change their health coverage.
Out of network
Providers without an agreement with your plan. Care may cost more, or not be covered at all.
Out-of-pocket maximum
The most you pay for covered care in a plan year. After you reach it, the plan pays covered costs in full.
P
Plan year
The twelve-month period a plan’s benefits, deductible and limits apply to.
Premium
The amount you pay, usually monthly, to keep your coverage active — whether or not you use care.
Preventive care
Routine care such as screenings, vaccinations and check-ups, often covered at little or no cost.
Q
Qualifying event
A life change — such as a move, a new child or losing other cover — that can allow changes to coverage outside open enrollment.
R
Reimbursement arrangement
An employer benefit that repays eligible coverage costs up to a set allowance, according to the employer’s policy.
S
Specialist
A doctor focused on one area of medicine, such as cardiology or dermatology. Some plans require a referral.
Supplemental cover
Optional insurance, such as dental, vision, accident or critical illness, added alongside major medical cover.
W
Waiting period
Time after enrollment before certain benefits, such as major dental work, become available.
