Short-Term Cover

Short gaps need thoughtful answers.

Short-term cover is designed for brief, well-understood gaps between longer-term plans. It can be useful in the right moment — and it is not a substitute for comprehensive coverage.

The use case

Useful in a gap, never a full plan.

Short-term plans are typically limited in what they cover and for how long. They may exclude pre-existing conditions, cap benefits and leave out services that comprehensive plans include. Sanaira presents them only alongside a clear long-term plan.

Read every limitation

Short-term policies vary widely and may not be available everywhere. Suitability depends on your health, your location and local rules. This page is educational, not a recommendation.

Typical moments

When a short gap usually appears.

Short-term cover is most often considered in three transitions — each with a known end date.

  1. Between jobs

    Your previous cover has ended and a new employer plan has a waiting period.

  2. Moving

    You are relocating and your new long-term options begin after the move.

  3. Waiting for a start date

    Another benefit — a new plan or programme — begins on a known future date.

Review carefully

Four things to read before you rely on it.

  1. Exclusions

    What is not covered, including pre-existing conditions and specific services.

  2. Duration

    How long the policy lasts and whether there is a hard end date.

  3. Renewals

    Whether it can be renewed, and whether terms change when it is.

  4. Your care needs

    Ongoing prescriptions or treatment may not be covered at all.
