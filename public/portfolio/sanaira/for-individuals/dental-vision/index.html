Dental & Vision

Everyday care deserves a clearer place in your plan.

Dental and vision cover handle the care you use most often — cleanings, eye exams, glasses. They are usually separate from health insurance, and worth understanding on their own terms.

Dental

Dental cover, in simple terms.

Most dental plans group care into prevention, restoration and an annual limit. Knowing which group your care falls in tells you most of what you need.

  1. Preventive visits

    Cleanings and exams, often twice a year, frequently covered at or near 100%.

  2. Restorations

    Fillings, crowns and similar work, usually shared between you and the plan.

  3. Annual maximum

    The most the plan will pay in a year. Costs beyond it are yours.

Vision

Vision cover, without the fine print.

Vision plans are simpler: an exam, lenses, and an allowance for frames or contacts — usually one or the other each year.

  1. Eye exams

    A routine exam each year or two, typically with a small copay.

  2. Lenses

    Standard lenses covered, with upgrades such as coatings at extra cost.

  3. Frames & contacts

    An allowance toward frames or contact lenses, often in place of each other.

In your quote

Add it to the plan, see the real total.

Dental and vision sits beside the health plan as one optional layer — cleanings, fillings, an eye exam and lenses each year — and the monthly estimate updates the moment you add it, so you can weigh it against paying per visit.

How comparison works

Before you add it

Five questions worth answering first.

A dental or vision plan earns its place when the care you expect costs more than the premium. These questions help you check, honestly, before you commit.

  • How often do you visit? Two cleanings and one eye exam a year is a very different picture from occasional care.
  • Is major work likely? Crowns, implants or orthodontics may involve waiting periods.
  • What is the annual maximum? Compare it with the care you expect, not the care you hope to avoid.
  • Are your providers included? Dental and vision networks differ from your medical network.
  • Does the premium beat paying directly? For light users, paying per visit can sometimes cost less.
