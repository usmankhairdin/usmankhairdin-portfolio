Compare Plans

Compare the whole year, not just the monthly price.

A plan’s monthly premium is only the visible part of its cost. Sanaira compares plans by how a real year might unfold — the doctors you keep, the medicines you take and what happens if the year gets harder.

Start a guided comparisonBrowse sample plans

What we compare

Five dimensions, one honest answer.

A useful comparison looks at the plan from your side of the table. These five questions turn a list of plans into a short, ranked shortlist.

Brush up on plan terms

  1. Your doctors

    Is each doctor and clinic you rely on in the plan’s network?

    Network

  2. Your prescriptions

    Which tier is each medicine on, and what does a refill cost?

    Formulary

  3. Expected care

    How many visits, tests and procedures does a normal year hold?

    Usage

  4. Deductible exposure

    How much could you pay before the plan begins to share costs?

    Risk

  5. Total yearly cost

    Premiums plus likely care costs, in a typical year and a harder one.

    The answer

Metal levels

Bronze to Platinum, as a trade-off.

Metal labels describe how a plan splits cost between the monthly premium and the care you use. Treat them as starting categories, not verdicts.

Bronze

Lowest monthly cost, highest cost when you use care.

Silver

A middle path for moderate, predictable care.

Gold

Higher monthly cost, lighter costs at the visit.

Platinum

Highest monthly cost, the least to pay when you need care.

A better question to ask.

The lowest premium often carries the highest risk if care needs rise. Comparing both years shows how much protection each extra dollar of premium actually buys.

In this example, Willow Complete costs a little more in a typical year, but around $1,460 less than Willow Everyday in a harder one.
